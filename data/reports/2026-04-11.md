## Top Stories

**Middle East Water Infrastructure Under Threat** — Desalination plants across the Middle East are becoming critical targets in regional conflicts, with Iran accusing the US of attacking a facility on Qeshm Island that supplies water to 30 villages, while Bahrain and Kuwait report damage to their own plants. This is particularly concerning given that 83% of the Middle East faces extreme water stress, projected to reach 100% by 2050, and the region hosts 27% of global desalination facilities despite having only 6% of world population. Qatar exemplifies this dependency, producing 77% of its fresh water and 99% of drinking water through desalination. [MIT Technology Review]

**Artemis II Returns Successfully** — NASA's Artemis II astronauts have safely returned to Earth after completing a historic flight around the moon, traveling farther from Earth than any humans before them. The mission's success was particularly significant given that the reentry phase—where the crew traveled at 32 times the speed of sound—represents a critical test that has caused entire space programs to be canceled when failed. The successful completion keeps the Artemis program on track for future lunar missions. [Wired]

**Enterprise Security Faces Multi-Platform Challenge** — Security operations centers are struggling with increasingly sophisticated attacks that span Windows, MacBooks, Linux, and mobile devices, creating fragmented workflows that leave critical gaps. Simultaneously, enterprise identity and access management systems are approaching a breaking point as "Identity Dark Matter" exists outside centralized visibility across thousands of applications, with IBM reporting average data breach costs of $4.4 million. [The Hacker News]

**AI Agents Drive Business Transformation** — Companies are preparing for dramatic increases in AI technology budgets—expected to grow over 70% in the next two years—as they shift toward "agent-first" enterprises where AI agents execute entire workflows autonomously rather than serving as bolt-on tools to legacy processes. This represents a fundamental redesign where humans become governors and agents become operators. [MIT Technology Review]
